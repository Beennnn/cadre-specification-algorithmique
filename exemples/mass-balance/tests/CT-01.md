## The data

`target_batch_mass = 1.000 kg`, `balance_step = 0.001 kg`

| Component | Fraction | Nominal | Rounded | **Weighed** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CMP-A` | 0.333333 | 0.333333 | 0.333 | **0.333** |
| `CMP-B` | 0.333333 | 0.333333 | 0.333 | **0.333** |
| `CMP-C` | 0.333334 | 0.333334 | 0.333 | **0.334** |

Sum of rounded `= 0.999` → `residual = +0.001` → goes to `CMP-C`, the single largest
fraction. **Final sum = 1.000 kg, exactly.**

## Why the case exists

It is the ordinary case, and it is aimed at the ordinary mistake: **rounding each dose and
stopping there**. Such an implementation weighs 0.999 kg of a 1.000 kg batch and reports
success. Nothing is out of range, no dose is wrong by more than a step, and the error is
one part in a thousand — invisible on a single batch, systematic across a production run.

`INV-01` is what catches it, and `INV-01` is stated at **strict equality** for that reason:
a tolerance of "one step" would have let this through by design.

The three fractions are near-equal but not equal, so there is a single largest one and
`RG-050` has an unambiguous answer. That is deliberate: the ambiguous version is
[CT-03](CT-03.md), and separating the two means a failure here and a failure there point at
different lines of code.

## What it does not test

| | |
|---|---|
| The tie-break | There is one strict maximum. See [CT-03](CT-03.md) |
| The sign of the residual | It is positive here. A `Math.abs` somewhere would survive — see [CT-02](CT-02.md) |
| The residual bound | One step, far under `P-02 = 3`. See [CT-05](CT-05.md) and [CT-09](CT-09.md) |
| The rounding mode | No nominal mass falls on a half-step, so `HALF_EVEN` is never arbitrated. See [CT-10](CT-10.md) |
