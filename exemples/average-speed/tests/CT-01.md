## The data

| Leg | Distance | Speed | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 km | 30.0 km/h | 2.000000 h |
| 2 | 60 km | 60.0 km/h | 1.000000 h |

```
total_distance = 60 + 60           = 120 km
total_duration = 60÷30 + 60÷60     = 3 h
average_speed  = 120 ÷ 3           = 40.0 km/h
```

**Expected: `average_speed` = 40.0 km/h.**

## Why the case exists

It is aimed at one specific wrong implementation: the **arithmetic mean of the leg
speeds**, `(30 + 60) ÷ 2 = 45.0 km/h`.

That mistake is not careless. "Average speed" reads as "the average of the speeds", and a
developer with no specification in hand will write it. Nothing in the code will look wrong
afterwards — the number is plausible, it sits between the two leg speeds, it varies in the
right direction.

The distances are equal here so that the two readings pull apart as far as they can: equal
distances mean the slow leg takes twice as long as the fast one, and therefore weighs twice
as much. **You spend more time on the slow legs, so they count more.**

The `INV-01` check passes for both the right and the wrong answer — 45.0 also lies between
30 and 60. An invariant that a wrong implementation satisfies is not useless, but it is not
what catches this one. **This case is.**
