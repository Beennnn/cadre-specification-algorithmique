## What it covers

| Item | |
|---|---|
| [`E-WTH-002`](../spec/SPEC-WTH-001.en.md#e-wth-002) | A `recorded_at` falls outside `observation_date` → reject |

## The data

`observation_date = 2026-01-15`. Seven readings, the last one dated **2026-01-16 at 00:00**:

| Time | 00:00 | 01:00 | 02:00 | 03:00 | 04:00 | 05:00 | **2026-01-16 00:00** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| °C | 2.0 | 1.5 | 0.5 | −0.5 | −1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 |

All `VALID`, all in range, all at distinct instants, seven of them — above `P-03` = 6.
**Expected: rejected with `E-WTH-002`.**

## Why the case exists

Because the summary claims to describe **one day**, and nothing in the result says which.

Look at the output contract: `station_id`, counts, temperatures, episodes, a label. There
is no date field. [`RG-005`](../spec/SPEC-WTH-001.en.md#rg-005--what-the-summary-identifies)
explains why — the day is `observation_date`, and `E-WTH-002` is the guarantee that makes
that true. Remove the check and the summary silently describes a day and a bit, with no
field anywhere that would show it.

The offending reading is **midnight of the next day**, on purpose. It is the one that a
range check written as "between the start and the end of the day, inclusive" lets
through — an off-by-one at the boundary that a reading at, say, 14:00 the next day would
never expose.

> **Two readings, two different failures.** A reading a day late is `E-WTH-002`; two
> readings at one instant is [`E-WTH-001`](CT-05.md). Both are about `recorded_at`, both
> are preconditions, and both would otherwise produce a plausible summary. Neither would be
> found by looking at the published numbers.

## What it does not test

| | |
|---|---|
| The other boundary | A reading from 23:59 the *previous* day is equally invalid and no case covers it |
| Time zones | Everything here is UTC, as the input contract states. What happens to a station reporting local time is not this specification's problem, and it is not tested |
| Any nominal behaviour | The calculation is never entered |
