## What it covers

| Item | |
|---|---|
| [`E-WTH-001`](../spec/SPEC-WTH-001.en.md#e-wth-001) | Two readings carry the same `recorded_at` → reject |

## The data

Seven readings, **two of them at 00:00**:

| Time | 00:00 | 00:00 | 01:00 | 02:00 | 03:00 | 04:00 | 05:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| °C | 2.0 | 1.5 | 0.5 | −0.5 | −1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 |

Every reading is `VALID` and in range; seven is above `P-03` = 6. **Expected: rejected with
`E-WTH-001`.**

## Why the case exists

Because [`RG-050`](../spec/SPEC-WTH-001.en.md#rg-050--frost-episodes) says *the first of the
run*, and with two readings at one instant that phrase names two different readings. **The
rule stops being a rule.**

That is what makes this a business rejection rather than input hygiene. Nothing crashes if
the check is missing: the day computes, the frost episodes come out, and `started_at` is
whichever of the two the sort happened to put first. Java's `List.sort` is stable, so it
would be whichever arrived first — reintroducing the dependence on arrival order that
[`INV-04`](../spec/SPEC-WTH-001.en.md#inv-04) forbids and that `EX-03` forbids for thirty
years of replay.

The duplicate is placed **first** on purpose. An implementation that validates lazily while
building its working set has already accepted one of the two by the time it meets the
other — and the specification places this in the preconditions precisely so that no reading
is ever accepted from a request that will be refused.

> **The check is on the raw input, before `RG-010` filters anything.** A duplicate instant
> is a defect of the acquisition chain, not a reading to discard: if one of the two were
> `FAULTY` and quietly dropped, the request would go through and the chain's fault would
> never be reported.

## What it does not test

| | |
|---|---|
| More than two at one instant | Only a pair is exercised |
| A duplicate among discarded readings | Both duplicates here are `VALID`. Whether a duplicate on two `FAULTY` readings should also reject is settled by the rule — it does — and tested by nothing |
| Any nominal behaviour | The calculation is never entered |
