## The data

The same day as [CT-01](CT-01.md), **without the 07:00 reading**.

| 00:00 | 01:00 | 02:00 | 03:00 | 04:00 | 05:00 | 06:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 1.5 | 0.5 | −0.5 | −1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 |

Round 1 — mean 0.5000, deviation 1.0801, threshold ±2.1602 → nothing discarded,
**converged immediately**.

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| `retained_count` / `rejected_count` | **7** / **0** |
| `mean_temperature` | **0.5 °C** |
| `frost_episodes` | one: 03:00 → 04:00, 2 readings |
| `quality_label` | **`GOOD`** |

## Why the case exists

For the equality with CT-01, and for the `GOOD` label.

**The mean is 0.5 °C in both cases.** CT-01 has an eighth reading at 45.0 °C and publishes
the same number as CT-02, which does not. That is not a coincidence to be noticed — it is
the **proof that the rejection worked**. An implementation that filters by flag and range
but never clips publishes `48.5 ÷ 8 = 6.1 °C` for CT-01, and 0.5 °C here. Read alone, 6.1
looks like a number. Read against this case, it is obviously wrong.

**It is also the only case labelled `GOOD`.** `RG-060` has three branches and each needs a
case: `ACCEPTABLE` is CT-01, `SUSPECT` is CT-04, `GOOD` is here and nowhere else. Take it
away and an implementation that never emits `GOOD` passes.

> **A control case is not a redundant case.** CT-02 detects nothing by itself: it is passed
> by every implementation that passes CT-01. Its value lies entirely in being *compared*,
> and that is why the two documents point at each other.
