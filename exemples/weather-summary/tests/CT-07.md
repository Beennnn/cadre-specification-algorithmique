## The data

Eight hourly readings, all `VALID`, nothing discarded:

| 00:00 | 01:00 | 02:00 | 03:00 | 04:00 | 05:00 | 06:00 | 07:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.3 | 0.4 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 0.5 |

They sum to **2.0 °C** over eight readings, so the mean is **exactly 0.25 °C** — on the tie.

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| `retained_count` / `rejected_count` | **8** / **0** |
| `mean_temperature` | **0.2 °C** |
| `min_temperature` / `max_temperature` | **0.0 °C** / **0.5 °C** |
| `frost_episodes` | **none** |
| `quality_label` | **`GOOD`** |

## Why the case exists

For two reasons, and the second one was not planned.

**`P-04` was arbitrated by nothing.** The rounding mode was chosen by the network manager,
given an effective date, implemented as a named constant, and listed as covered. Six cases
ran through `RG-040` and every one of them had a mean that no change of mode could move:
0.5 in CT-01 and CT-02, 0.4571… in CT-04. Swap `HALF_EVEN` for `HALF_UP` and the whole set
still passed. Here the two modes part company — `HALF_EVEN` publishes **0.2**, the even
neighbour; `HALF_UP` would publish 0.3.

**And then the case would not hold still.** Written and run, it published 0.2 on some runs
and 0.3 on others. The permutation property found it: reordering the same eight readings
changed the published mean.

The cause is not the rounding mode. **Double-precision addition is not associative.** The
readings are one-decimal values with no exact binary form; summed in one order they give
2.0, summed in another 2.0000000000000004. Divided by eight that is 0.25 against
0.2500000000000001 — one is on the tie, the other is a hair above it, and `HALF_EVEN` sends
them to 0.2 and 0.3 respectively.

> **This is a defect in `EX-01`, not in the code.** The requirement said double precision
> was enough because readings carry an uncertainty of ±0.2 °C, far above any numerical
> noise. That reasoning holds for a number you *compare*; it fails for a number you
> **round**, because rounding has no tolerance at all — it turns a difference of 10⁻¹⁶ into
> a difference of 0.1 °C in the published value.
>
> Since v1.1.0 `EX-01` says so, and `RG-040` says what to do: the published mean is
> accumulated in **exact decimal** arithmetic, where the sum of one-decimal readings is
> exact and the division rounds correctly by construction. The statistics of `RG-030` stay
> in double — they feed a comparison against a threshold, not a published number.

**No other case in this repository would have found it.** The invariant is a property over
all inputs, checked on generated reorderings; the tie is the one place where the property
has any teeth. Either device alone finds nothing.

## What it does not test

| | |
|---|---|
| A tie that rounds **up** | 0.25 goes down to the even neighbour. A mean of 0.35 would go up to 0.4, and no case covers that direction |
| The rejection loop | Nothing is discarded, so `RG-030` runs one round and stops |
| Frost episodes | Every reading is `≥ 0.0 °C` |
| Whether double precision is enough elsewhere | The clipping statistics are still in double. `EX-01` now claims that is fine — **and no case verifies that claim** |
