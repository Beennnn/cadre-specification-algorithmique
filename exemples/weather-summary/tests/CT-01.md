## The data

Station `STA-001`, 2026-01-15, hourly from 00:00 UTC, every reading flagged `VALID`.

| 00:00 | 01:00 | 02:00 | 03:00 | 04:00 | 05:00 | 06:00 | 07:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 1.5 | 0.5 | −0.5 | −1.0 | 0.0 | 1.0 | **45.0** |

**Round 1** — mean 6.0625, sample deviation 15.7649, threshold ±31.5297 → **45.0
discarded**.
**Round 2** — mean 0.5000, deviation 1.0801, threshold ±2.1602 → nothing discarded,
**converged**.

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| `retained_count` / `rejected_count` | **7** / **1** |
| `mean_temperature` | **0.5 °C** |
| `min_temperature` / `max_temperature` | **−1.0 °C** / **2.0 °C** |
| `frost_episodes` | **one**: 03:00 → 04:00, 2 readings |
| `quality_label` | **`ACCEPTABLE`** |

## Why the case exists

It settles two different things, and both are the kind that get decided silently.

**A 45.0 °C reading in January carries a `VALID` flag.** The acquisition chain sees nothing
wrong — the sensor returned a number in range, the transmission was clean. Only `RG-030`
catches it, and only because the rule looks at the *dispersion of the day*, not at absolute
plausibility. Without this case, an implementation that filters and never clips passes the
whole set: every other case has no outlier to find.

**05:00 at exactly 0.0 °C is not frost.** The episode ends at 04:00. `P-06` is a strict
threshold, and this reading sits exactly on it: a `≤` would extend the episode to 05:00 and
add one reading to its count. The network publishes frost warnings — with `≤` it would
alert on every autumn night that touches zero.

The two facts are in one case on purpose: they concern different rules and different halves
of the algorithm, and a failure names which.
